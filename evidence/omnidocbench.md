**Vendor capture.** OpenDataLab is a group inside Shanghai AI Laboratory, a "national-level new-type research institute" the Shanghai municipal government stood up at WAIC 2020 (https://merics.org/en/comment/profile-shanghai-ai-lab-driving-both-ai-safety-and-development). OpenDataLab authors OmniDocBench AND builds MinerU, which ranks top-2 on its own leaderboard, 95.7-96.1 (https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04771). Referee and leading competitor are the same org.

**Methodology.** A public tracker shows 16/16 current leaderboard rows are vendor self-reported, 0 independently verified (https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/omnidocbench-1.5). An independent audit, PureDocBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07492), found 2,580 ground-truth annotation errors: 12.08% of 21,353 scored blocks. MinerU2.5-Pro's own paper attributes its score jump (92.98→95.69) purely to a "Data Engine" scaling training data 10M→65.5M samples (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04771): training-data optimization by the lab that also defines the eval taxonomy: a soft teach-to-the-test surface even absent literal leakage. GitHub issue #120 shows 11 pipelines (one scoring 0.646) silently dropped between v1 and v1.5, unexplained, still open (https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench/issues/120).

**Saturation.** Named and dated: LlamaIndex's Jerry Liu published "OmniDocBench is Saturated" Feb 2026 (https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/omnidocbench-is-saturated-what-s-next-for-ocr-benchmarks). Top-6 models cluster within 1.7 points (94.49-96.19); trackers now treat scores above ~94 as noise (https://instavar.com/blog/ai-production-stack/OCR_SOTA_Feb_2026_Open_Document_AI_Leaderboard).

**Gaming history.** No named cheating scandal surfaced, but the mechanism is wide open: self-report-only submission, zero independent verification, the benchmark's own operator fielding a top entrant, and unexplained result drops.

**Liveness.** Real, fast cadence confirms the prior signal: v1.5 (2025-09-25) → v1.6 (2026-04-10) → v1.7 (2026-04-30) (https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench). But mirrors disagree on "current leader", MiniMax M3 0.916 vs GLM-OCR 94.6 vs MinerU 96.1, depending on version tracked, so circulating numbers aren't a single stable thing.

**Case for.** Real annotated documents (1,651 pages, 10 types), peer-reviewed (CVPR 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07626), still separates competent parsers from weak/generalist ones by wide margins outside the saturated top cluster (e.g., Gemini 3 Pro trails specialist parsers by ~4+ points): a reasonable floor test for "can this tool parse a document at all," not a frontier leaderboard.

VERDICT: caveat | OmniDocBench | Self-reported-only leaderboard (0/16 verified), operator's own MinerU tops it, saturated top cluster; floor test only, not ranking.
